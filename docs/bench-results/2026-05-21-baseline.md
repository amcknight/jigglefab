Pre-Phase-2 baseline. This is the table Phase 2's GPU-CCD work will be measured against.

**Machine:** AMD Ryzen 7 9800X3D 8-Core, Windows 11 (build 26200)
**Build:** `cargo run --release --bin bench`
**Args used:** `--warmup 5 --frames 600 --max-wall-seconds 30 --csv …baseline.csv`
**Sim version:** commit `b4e4613` (post-Task-10). Table re-rendered with `e1839ba` semantics — `bonds_lost` only, see investigation note below.

> **Note on args:** the spec defaults are `--warmup 60 --frames 3000 --max-wall-seconds 300`. At our current scheduler, those defaults make a full sweep take hours (chains_50x30 alone would spend ~8 min in warmup before timing). For this first baseline we used `--warmup 5 --frames 600 --max-wall-seconds 30` — short enough to iterate, long enough to surface the cliff. The Phase-2-after-bench should re-run at spec defaults once the scheduler can sustain them.

## Observations

**Where the cliff is.** chains_10x30 (300 beads) runs at 554 fps with 92 substeps of 16ms-budget headroom — the deployed sim before this branch sits here. Add 3× the chains (chains_30x30, 900 beads, same density) and we crater to 2.6 fps. Triple it again (chains_100x30, 3000 beads) and a single frame takes 42 seconds. The wall is somewhere between 300 and 900 beads at constant density; sublinear chain growth (chains_10x100, 1000 beads but distributed across just 10 chains) holds 40 fps, so the wall tracks contact-density more than raw bead count.

**Real bond loss is small but non-zero in the deep wall.** chains_50x30, chains_5x300, and chains_100x30 lose 2–6 originally-bonded pairs (`bonds_lost > 0`) — these are real correctness failures where a bonded pair drifted past R without the scheduler catching the exit. The Haskell escape demon, manifesting at a small but nonzero rate. `iter_cap` is *not* hit in any of these (`iter_cap_sat ≈ 0`), so it's not the iteration budget — likely a CCD edge case under dense contact pressure that misses an exit and `enforce_bonds` is too coarse to recover.

The initial baseline (commit `9793a68`) reported much larger violations using a `bonds_lost / bonds_added` framing. An investigation on 2026-05-21 (max-`|v|` probe + offending-pair identity) showed that `bonds_added` was conflating two unrelated things: real scheduler failures (small) and natural chain folding — non-adjacent intra-chain pairs (e.g. beads 9 and 11 of the same chain) being within R of each other once the chain bends back on itself. Wire chemistry has no angular stiffness, so chains fold freely under load; the resulting geometric proximity of skip-one pairs is physics, not a bug. The metric now reports only `bonds_lost` in the headline (`n (-N)` notation); `bonds_added` remains in the CSV as raw data but is not a correctness signal.

Max `|v|` during the probe stayed at ~2 across all scenarios — nowhere near the ~30 threshold for grid tunneling. Tunneling is *not* the failure mode; it's a CCD logic edge case at high contact density that we'd need finer instrumentation to localize.

**Heavy-tailed substep distribution.** Look at chains_30x30's substep_us: mean 39ms, p99 3.9ms — the mean is 10× the p99. Most substeps are fast; a small minority of "knot" substeps are dragging the average up by 100×. This pattern is the scheduler's signature failure mode: dense pile-ups serialize through the sequential contact-resolution loop.
