# Bench baseline — 2026-05-21

Pre-Phase-2 baseline. This is the table Phase 2's GPU-CCD work will be measured against.

**Machine:** AMD Ryzen 7 9800X3D 8-Core, Windows 11 (build 26200)
**Build:** `cargo run --release --bin bench`
**Args used:** `--warmup 5 --frames 600 --max-wall-seconds 30 --csv …baseline.csv`
**Sim version:** commit `b4e4613` (post-Task-10, pre-Task-13 deploy bump)

> **Note on args:** the spec defaults are `--warmup 60 --frames 3000 --max-wall-seconds 300`. At our current scheduler, those defaults make a full sweep take hours (chains_50x30 alone would spend ~8 min in warmup before timing). For this first baseline we used `--warmup 5 --frames 600 --max-wall-seconds 30` — short enough to iterate, long enough to surface the cliff. The Phase-2-after-bench should re-run at spec defaults once the scheduler can sustain them.

## Observations

**Where the cliff is.** chains_10x30 (300 beads) runs at 554 fps with 92 substeps of 16ms-budget headroom — the deployed sim before this branch sits here. Add 3× the chains (chains_30x30, 900 beads, same density) and we crater to 2.6 fps. Triple it again (chains_100x30, 3000 beads) and a single frame takes 42 seconds. The wall is somewhere between 300 and 900 beads at constant density; sublinear chain growth (chains_10x100, 1000 beads but distributed across just 10 chains) holds 40 fps, so the wall tracks contact-density more than raw bead count.

**Bonds breaking in the deep wall.** Three scenarios at 1500+ beads (`chains_50x30`, `chains_5x300`, `chains_100x30`) failed the bond-conservation invariant — they finished with both *fewer* bonds (chains lost integrity) and *more* bonds (cross-chain bonds formed) than they started with. This is the "Haskell escape demon" the engine design doc explicitly named as motivation for CCD: when the scheduler can't drain all contacts before the substep budget runs out, beads pass through what should be barriers and bond with neighbours they shouldn't touch. The `iter_cap_sat` rate is near-zero in these cases, so it's not the `N×64` iteration cap that's getting hit — it's just that each frame is taking 13+ seconds, and the warmup positions drift far enough that bond topology is corrupted before the timed phase even begins.

**Heavy-tailed substep distribution.** Look at chains_30x30's substep_us: mean 39ms, p99 3.9ms — the mean is 10× the p99. Most substeps are fast; a small minority of "knot" substeps are dragging the average up by 100×. This pattern is the scheduler's signature failure mode: dense pile-ups serialize through the sequential contact-resolution loop.
